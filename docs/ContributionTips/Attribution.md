## 1. Credit formal sources

Credit vendored Mathlib PR material, copied or closely adapted formalizations, and the central declarations a construction follows. You don't need to credit every Mathlib dependency, which every proof has.

<a id="credit-informal-sources"></a>

## 2. Credit informal sources

Credit the paper, textbook, lecture notes, or Zulip discussion your work follows. Physics results usually have a standard textbook or paper reference, so add one wherever a reader would expect it.

<a id="add-references-to-the-bibliography"></a>

## 3. Add references to the bibliography

Physlib keeps references in `docs/references.bib` and cites them in the `## iv. References` section of the module docstring. Add your sources there, in the code and not only in the PR description.

<a id="credit-what-you-follow-closely"></a>

## 4. Credit what you follow closely

Similarity to an identifiable source in theorem order, notation, or proof plan is enough to call for credit, even when no text is copied.

<a id="routine-work-needs-no-attribution"></a>

## 5. Routine work needs no attribution

Routine work that draws on nothing in particular does not need an attribution.

<a id="check-your-references"></a>

## 6. Check your references

Bibliographic correctness, such as whether a work exists and whether the page numbers are right, is your responsibility as the author under `AI-POLICY.md` §2.1. Check every reference yourself, especially any suggested by an AI.
